The law of the burnt offering    Message 23
I. The burnt offering being upon the hearth (firewood) on the altar all night until the morning
A. Signifying that anything offered as a burnt offering must be put on the place of burning to be burned
B. What is offered should remain at the burning place through the dark night until morning
II. The fire on the altar being kept burning continually and not going out
A. Signifying that God as the burning fire in the universe is ready to receive (burn) what is offered to Him as food
B. Signifying that God’s desire to accept what is offered to Him never ceases
III. The priest burning wood on the altar every morning
IV. The priest putting on his linen garment and linen breeches over his flesh in taking up the ashes of the burnt offering
V. The priest putting on other garments to carry the ashes outside the camp
VI. The priest arranging the burnt offering on the altar and burning the fat portions of the peace offering on it
A. Signifying that we should have the burning of our burnt offering laid as a foundation for our sweet fellowship with God
B. Signifying that on the foundation of the burnt offering our peace offering should be burned for a fragrance to God
The law of the meal offering    Message 24
I. The meal offering to be offered before Jehovah in front of the altar
II. The portion of the meal offering to Aaron and his sons to be eaten in a holy place, in the court of the tent of meeting
A. Signifying that we enjoy Christ as our meal offering (life supply for our service) without sin
B. Signifying that we enjoy Christ in a separated, sanctified realm
C. Signifying that we enjoy Christ in the sphere of the church
III. Not baking the meal offering with leaven
IV. Considering the meal offering most holy. like the sin offering and the trespass offering
A. Signifying that to enjoy Christ as the life supply we need to deal with sin in our fallen nature
B. Signifying that to enjoy Christ as the life supply we also need to deal with the sins in our conduct
V. Every male among the sons of Aaron eating of the meal offering
A. Signifying that the partakers of Christ as the life supply should be strong in life
B. Signifying that the partakers of Christ should be God’s serving ones — priests
VI. The meal offering of Aaron and his sons on the day when aaron is anointed
A. The tenth of an ephah of fine flour as a continual meal offering
B. Half of the meal offering in the morning and half in the evening to keep it a continual offering
C. The meal offering being entirely burned and not eaten by the priest

Discernment in diet    Message 36
I. The significance of eating
A. To contact the things outside of us that could affect us inside
B. To receive things from the outside that can be digested inside to become our constituent expressed in our living
II. The categories of living creatures that are clean with respect to diet
A. Beasts that divide the hoof and chew the cud
B. Aquatic animals having fins and scales
C. Birds that have wings for flying and that eat seeds of life as their food supply
D. Insects having wings and having jointed legs above their feet for leaping on the ground
III. The categories of living creatures that are not clean with respect to diet
A. Beasts that do not divide the hoof and beasts that go on their paws
B. Aquatic animals that do not have fins and scales
C. Birds that eat flesh and carcasses as their food supply
D. Insects that have four feet for creeping about
E. Creatures that creep on the earth or go on their belly or on four feet, or have many feet of all the creeping things that creep on the earth
Abstaining from death    Message 37
I. All dead things being unclean, and death being more defiling and abominable than sin
A. Through a trespass offering any sin shall be forgiven immediately
B. A person touching the carcass of any animal shall be unclean until the evening, and a person touching the carcass of any man shall be unclean seven days
II. A person defiled by the carcass of any animal shall wash his clothes
III. Anything upon which the carcass of any animal falls is unclean, and anything thus defiled shall be washed in water
IV. The carcass of any animal falling into an earthen vessel or upon an oven or a stove makes it unclean, and it shall be broken
V. Any food in the earthen vessel upon which water comes and any drink in the vessel shall be made unclean by the fallen carcass
VI. A spring or a cistern holding water shall remain clean
VII. If any part of the carcass of an animal falls on any seed for sowing, the seed is clean
VIII. The conclusion
A. Christ is the reality of cleanness
B. Only Christ and all that is out of Christ are clean for our food supply
C. In terms of typology:
1. Christ divides the hoof and chews the cud
2. Christ has fins and scales
3. Christ has jointed legs above the feet and leaps on the ground
4. Christ is a spring or a cistern that holds living water
5. Christ is a seed for sowing
D. Christ is the supply in our daily life in every aspect
E. Only Christ is able to keep us holy, as God is holy
Uncleanness in human birth    Message 38
I. A woman’s relationship to human birth
A. In figure, woman representing all mankind
1. In God’s economy all mankind being female
2. The uncleanness within the woman signifying the uncleanness within all mankind
B. The human birth coming out of the woman being unclean, signifying that:
1. The source of all mankind is unclean
2. The uncleanness of mankind is from within
II. The uncleanness of the male’s birth being for seven days, and that of the female’s being for fourteen days
III. After the birth of a male, the woman remaining in the uncleanness of the blood for thirty-three days, and after the birth of a female, for sixty-six days
